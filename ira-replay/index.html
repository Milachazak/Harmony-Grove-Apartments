The Playbook

What Jeff covered

The evening moved from what is allowed to how it is done. Here is the path, in the order he walked it.

01
What a self-directed account is
Since ERISA in 1974 a retirement plan has been able to own almost anything. The IRS names only three things it cannot hold: life insurance contracts, S-corporation stock and collectibles. The big custodians limit you to their menu of public assets as a business decision, not a legal one. A self-directed custodian is regulated the same way but is willing to hold the rest.
02
What it can own
Private funds and private companies, real estate and land, oil and gas, precious metals, cryptocurrency, tax liens, promissory notes and debt-leveraged real estate. The creative end of the list included Super Bowl tickets bought and resold inside a Roth IRA, and a co-founder's Roth IRA that raised cattle.
03
Six plans you can self-direct
Traditional IRA (tax-deferred) and Roth IRA (tax-free growth, pay tax on the seed, not the crop). SEP IRA and Solo 401(k) for the self-employed, with far higher limits and a Roth option in the 401(k). And the specialty plans: the HSA with its triple tax benefit, and the Coverdell ESA for education.
04
Why investors do it
True diversification away from assets that all move with the market. The tax shelter of a qualified plan. Investing in what you actually understand, the deal on the street you can name rather than the mutual fund you cannot. Passive by design, with an operator doing the work, and terms disclosed up front.
05
The rules
You and your IRA are not one and the same, so it cannot buy from you, lend to you or hold a property you already own. Disqualified persons run up and down the family tree: you, your spouse, parents, grandparents, children, grandchildren and their spouses. Siblings, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews and your in-laws are not disqualified.
06
Three steps to get started
Open online, processed in a business day. Fund by IRA-to-IRA transfer (about five to seven business days), by rollover from an old employer plan (two to three weeks, still by paper check) or by contribution. Then invest by submitting a Direction of Investment with the subscription documents titled to the custodian for the benefit of your IRA.
07
A real example
Jeff's own Roth IRA and HSA became the lender on a $100,000 private loan to a local investor: 75% from the Roth, 25% from the HSA, 10% simple interest paid monthly, two points at closing, an 18-month term, secured by a deed of trust. About 12% cash on cash, every payment back into the accounts tax-free, and the principal returned at the refinance.
08
Checkbook control and UBIT
An IRA-owned LLC gives you a checking account for high-volume or hands-on investing like auctions and rentals, but adds cost and complexity a passive fund investor does not need. And when a fund uses bank leverage, the debt-financed share of the return can owe unrelated business income tax, filed by the IRA on Form 990-T, with the first $1,000 exempt.
