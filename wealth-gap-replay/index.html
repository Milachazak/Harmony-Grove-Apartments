The Playbook

What Derick covered

First he showed how to find your number. Then he showed the system that gets you there. Here is the path in the order he walked it.

01
Know what you want
Start with the outcome. Write it as I want this income by this date working these hours. Derick's own number is $1.2 million a year on less than 15 hours a week. Then write down why. A number with no reason behind it will not hold up when things get hard.
02
Find your number
Use his one-page worksheet. Add your net worth without your home to the value of your business. Price the life you want in today's dollars after tax. Multiply by 1.33 to cover taxes. Then multiply again for inflation. At 15 years out the factor is 1.67.
03
Income beats net worth
A portfolio all in stocks supports about a 4% withdrawal. So you need 25 times the income you want. Spread across assets that move on their own you may draw 6% or 8%. Then you need 17 or 12.5 times. In his example the gap ran from $8.4 million to $18.1 million.
04
Time is on your side
Opportunity cost works for you too. Invest $50,000 a year for 30 years at 7% and you end near $5 million. About $3.5 million of that is growth. For many in the room that is about a tenth of their income.
05
Pillar 1: Tax strategy
Most people plan taxes in December or March. At your income that is too late. Forecast in January. Check in at midyear. Do the deep planning from late August into October. Then act on it by December 31. A CPA records your taxes. A strategist looks for ways to cut them.
06
Pillar 2: Growth and cash flow
High earners need liquidity and tax efficiency. Accredited investors also get access to private deals. Derick pointed to the endowment model. It spreads money across many asset classes that do not rise and fall together. That steadier mix supports a higher withdrawal rate.
07
Pillar 3: Systems and automation
Set up automatic transfers from every paycheck. Fund taxes and savings and investments in the background. Savings is the gateway drug to investing, he joked. One dentist client went from owing the IRS to holding over $250,000 in savings in two years.
08
Pillars 4 and 5: Protection and independence
Plan for death and disability and divorce before they happen. Keep cash and the right insurance. If you own a practice build one that runs without you. A business that needs you owns you. Buyers pay a higher multiple for one that does not.

A friend is a W-2 employee in New York City. They own no business. Maybe they own their home. What is the highest-yield tax move for them?

It depends on what the money needs to do. People invest for three reasons: tax breaks, cash flow or growth. Leveraged donation strategies can cut taxes a lot but create no income. What is the asset minimum to work with Derick's team?
Asked in the chat and read out by Addie Agarwal
There is none. The guide is income of about $300,000 a year. That is roughly where taxes get real and private investments open up. Early in a career Derick says to focus on growing what you earn. Once you pass $300,000 start shaving off money to put to work.
Is the accredited investor test based on gross income or net income?
Asked in the chat and read out by Addie Agarwal
Gross. It is your pre-tax income and not what you take home. You can also qualify with $1 million or more in assets not counting your home. The exact thresholds come from SEC rules. Check them with your advisor before you rely on them.
Kirk heard that most people work from January to June just to pay their taxes. Is it really that bad?
Raised by Dr. Kirk Campbell
In a place like Manhattan it is. Even with no state income tax a 39% federal bracket plus Social Security puts you near 41%. So you work until about May. The tax you save this year can fund next year's investments.
