What you will walk away with

Four things most people learn
ten years too late.

No market outlook, no slides about where rates are going. Just the mechanics of an account you are already allowed to open, explained by somebody who sets them up every day, and the handful of details that decide whether the whole thing works the way you expect.

01 · Scope

What your account is actually allowed to own

The tax code runs backwards from what most people assume. It names a short list of things a retirement account may not hold, and leaves nearly everything else open, real estate and private funds included. You will leave knowing exactly where the line sits.

02 · Transfer

How to move the money without a tax bill

Old 401(k)s and 403(b)s, existing IRAs, SEPs and SIMPLEs can move custodian to custodian with no taxable event. Jeff walks through the mechanics, the paperwork and the timing, including the small mistakes that quietly turn a clean transfer into a distribution.

03 · Compounding

What tax-advantaged compounding really does

Rent and any eventual sale proceeds are paid to the account rather than to you. In a Traditional IRA that growth is tax-deferred, and in a Roth it can be tax-free. Somewhere around year four that stops being an academic difference.

04 · Rules

The rules, before you need them

Prohibited transactions, disqualified persons, and the unrelated business income tax a mortgage can create inside an IRA. In plain English, with examples. This is the part that costs people money when they learn it late, and it is why the hour is worth your time even if you never invest a dollar alongside us.

The rest

Eight accounts that can be self-directed.

Check with your plan administrator

Traditional IRA

Transfer all or part of the balance into a self-directed account. Growth stays tax-deferred until you take distributions.

Roth IRA

Same mechanics, and qualified growth can come out tax-free. Beneficiaries inherit the same treatment.

SEP IRA

Built for the self-employed and small practices. It self-directs in exactly the same way as an IRA.

SIMPLE IRA

Common in smaller practices, and eligible once the plan's own holding period has been met.

Solo 401(k)

For the self-employed with no full-time staff. Traditional and Roth sides, chosen year by year.

Old 401(k) or 403(b)

From a hospital, practice or employer you have left. This is the single most common source of funding we see.

Health savings account

The three-way break: deductible going in, tax-free growth, and tax-free out for qualified medical costs. It can be self-directed too.

Coverdell ESA

An education account that grows and distributes tax-free for qualified education costs. Rarely mentioned, and eligible.

One caveat worth knowing up front: a 401(k) with your current employer usually cannot be moved while you are still there, though a growing number of plans allow an in-service rollover, sometimes for part of the balance. Your plan administrator can tell you in about five minutes, and it is the first thing to check.

Eligibility for the SEP, SIMPLE, solo 401(k), health savings account and Coverdell depends on your own circumstances, including self-employment, staff and, for the HSA, a qualifying high-deductible health plan. Contribution limits are indexed and change from year to year, so Jeff will cover the current figures live rather than us printing numbers here that go stale.

answers.

Register anyway. We send the recording to everyone on the list afterwards, and it goes up on our events page. That said, the Q&A is the part that does not really survive a recording, so come if you can.

Does moving my 401(k) trigger taxes?▾

Handled correctly, as a direct custodian to custodian transfer, no. Handled carelessly, it can. Telling those two apart is most of section three, and it is the single most useful twelve minutes of the evening.

Our deals carry a mortgage. Does that create a tax bill inside my IRA?▾

It can, and we would rather you heard it from us. The debt-financed share of income inside an IRA can trigger unrelated business income tax. It is often small and sometimes it is not, and it depends entirely on your account and the deal. Jeff covers the rule in section four, and you should confirm your own position with your CPA before you commit to anything.
