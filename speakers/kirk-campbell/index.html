Signature talks

Programs built for rooms that want substance.

Every talk is tailored to the audience and the brief. These are the four most requested.

01
Keynote

Let Your Money Scrub In

Keynote, 30 to 60 min

Physicians spend a decade or more learning their craft and almost no time learning to own. Dr. Campbell makes the case that the modern physician's money should work as hard as they do, and shows what that looks like around a full clinical schedule.

  • Why a high income alone does not build wealth
  • The seven to ten income streams wealthy families keep
  • A first step that fits a clinical schedule
02
Keynote or fireside

My Real Estate Residency

Keynote or fireside, 30 to 45 min

A friend's syndication, a first quarterly check and a K-1 showing a tax loss changed how he saw money. He then trained himself the way surgeons train: coaches, mentors and years as a passive investor before his first deal as a general partner.

  • How to learn investing the way you learned medicine
  • What passive investors actually receive
  • When, and whether, to move from LP to GP
03
Keynote or panel

The Lost Ancillaries

Keynote or panel, 30 to 45 min

Practice real estate, surgery centers, imaging and other ancillaries once paid physicians quietly in the background. Hospital and private equity consolidation took most of that away. This talk maps what was lost and how real assets can rebuild it.

  • What consolidation took from physician income
  • Which assets can replace it, and their trade offs
  • How tax advantaged ownership fits a W-2 career
04
Workshop

Due Diligence Like a Surgeon

Workshop, 60 to 90 min

Surgeons look for risk and challenge assumptions for a living, and those are the same muscles a good investor uses. Every deal Dr. Campbell presents has to pass his mother's retirement account test. Here is how to run that test on any sponsor and any property.

  • The questions to ask any sponsor
  • Reading a deal for downside before upside
  • Red flags that should end the conversation

About

A surgeon's training, an owner's mindset.

Born in Jamaica, Kirk Campbell moved to Springfield, Massachusetts at nine, and by seven he already knew he wanted to be a doctor. A high school knee injury pointed him to sports medicine. He graduated magna cum laude from Union College, earned his MD at NYU, trained in orthopedic surgery at NYU and the Hospital for Joint Diseases, where he was executive chief resident, and completed a sports medicine fellowship at Rush, caring for the Chicago Bulls, the White Sox and DePaul athletes as an assistant team physician.

Today he is an Associate Professor of Orthopedic Surgery at NYU Grossman School of Medicine and a board certified surgeon at NYU Langone Health, specializing in the shoulder, elbow and knee. He has 143 research publications, and his commentary has appeared in PEOPLE, HuffPost and GOLF.com.

His second education began with a friend's apartment syndication. He spent years as a passive investor, his own real estate residency, before co-founding Mila Penn Capital with his wife, Rosanmi, and Mila Penn Chazak with J. Claude Mouaffi. In 2026 the firm raised $4 million in 65 days for a 75 unit community in Marietta, Georgia.
